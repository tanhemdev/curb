## Category Breakdown

| Category | Listings | % of Total | Conversion Rate | Avg Price |
|----------|----------|------------|-----------------|----------|
| Furniture | 370 | 45% | 71% | $18 |
| Electronics | 165 | 20% | 68% | $24 |
| Kitchen | 123 | 15% | 79% | $6 |
| Textbooks | 99 | 12% | 89% | $12 |
| Misc | 66 | 8% | 52% | $8 |

### Notable findings
- **Textbooks** had the highest conversion rate (89%). Small, easy to transport, clear value. Might deserve its own flow or search.
- **Kitchen items** converted well (79%) and were mostly free. People don't want to ship pots across the country.
- **Furniture** was the highest volume but not the highest conversion  -  probably because furniture is harder to transport (need a friend with a car).
- **Misc** (decor, random stuff) had the lowest conversion. Harder to browse for when you don't know what you need.

### Top items (by claim speed)
1. Mini fridge  -  avg 38 minutes to claim
2. Desk lamp  -  avg 52 minutes
3. Textbooks  -  avg 1.1 hours
4. Desk chair  -  avg 1.4 hours
5. Bookshelf  -  avg 2.1 hours

Mini fridges are apparently the most desired object in the college universe.

## Pricing

| Price Range | Listings | % | Conversion |
|-------------|----------|---|------------|
| Free ($0) | 312 | 38% | 91% |
| $1-$15 | 198 | 24% | 78% |
| $16-$30 | 167 | 20% | 67% |
| $31-$50 | 89 | 11% | 58% |
| $51+ | 57 | 7% | 42% |

Free items convert at 91%  -  nearly everything free gets claimed. This confirms the research finding that outgoing students mostly want stuff *gone*, not monetized. The platform's value is coordination, not commerce.

Worth noting: we don't handle payments. All transactions are cash/Venmo at handoff. Keeping money off-platform keeps us simple and avoids a ton of regulatory headaches. So far nobody has complained.

## User Satisfaction

Sent a post-pilot survey to all 214 users. Got 89 responses (42% response rate).

**NPS: 64**
- Promoters (9-10): 71%
- Passives (7-8): 22%
- Detractors (0-6): 7%

An NPS of 64 is really high but take it with a grain of salt  -  it's a small, self-selected sample and it was free.

**"Would you use Curb again next move-out?"**
- Definitely: 74%
- Probably: 19%
- Maybe: 5%
- No: 2%

**"What would you improve?"**
1. Better search (31 mentions)
2. More categories / sub-categories (18 mentions)
3. Ability to save/favorite items (15 mentions)
4. Notifications were inconsistent (12 mentions)
5. Would want a native app (8 mentions)
6. Need a way to relist expired items (7 mentions)

**Selected quotes:**
> "I furnished my entire room for $35. My roommate spent $400 at Target."

> "this is what Free & For Sale should have been"

> "I honestly felt emotional giving my desk to someone who was excited about it instead of leaving it by the dumpster"

> "Only issue was that search didn't work well  -  I was looking for a 'bookshelf' and couldn't find the ones listed as 'shelf' or 'shelving unit'"

3. This was a 7-day pilot with zero marketing budget

## What Broke

### Technical issues
1. **Firestore real-time listener over-fetching**  -  On May 10 (peak day), the listener was re-reading every listing on every update. Hit 90% of daily free-tier reads by 3pm. Fixed by narrowing the query and adding cursor-based updates. Scariest 20 minutes of the pilot.

2. **Large photo uploads hanging**  -  Two users tried uploading 20MB+ photos and the upload just spun forever. Added a 5MB client-side check with a helpful error. Should have caught this in testing.

3. **Push notification token expiry**  -  Some users stopped getting push notifications after 2-3 days. FCM tokens expire and need to be refreshed. Added token refresh logic but some users had already stopped checking.

4. **Pin drop accuracy on Android Chrome**  -  4 reports of pins landing in the wrong location. Turns out the Google Maps tap handler returns slightly different coordinates on some Android devices. Haven't fully fixed this yet.

### Non-technical issues
1. **One listing was borderline inappropriate**  -  Someone listed a "free mattress" with a photo that was... not great. Didn't violate any rules but made the feed look sketchy. Added this to the "need content moderation" backlog.

2. **Handoff no-shows**  -  23 cases where someone claimed an item, coordinated a pickup time, and then didn't show up. That's a 4% no-show rate, which is actually way better than Marketplace (anecdotally 20-30%), but still frustrating for sellers.

3. **Pricing disputes**  -  3 cases where the claimer showed up and the seller tried to charge more than listed. We don't have any enforcement mechanism for this. Something to think about.

## What Surprised Us

1. **The social element was unexpected.** We built a transactional marketplace. Users turned it into something slightly more human  -  thanking each other, sharing stories about items, one person wrote "take care of this desk, it got me through orgo." We didn't design for this but we should lean into it.

2. **Evening listings outperformed morning ones 2.3x.** Items listed between 6-10pm got claimed faster. Hypothesis: people browse after dinner when they're winding down. Might want to suggest optimal listing times.

3. **Bundle requests were common.** 14 chat conversations included someone asking "can I just take everything?" We don't have a bundle feature but clearly should.

4. **The counter was motivating.** Multiple survey respondents mentioned the "items diverted" counter on the feed as something that made them feel good about participating. Gamification via impact, not badges.

5. **We had 3 users from Stanford.** They signed up with `stanford.edu` emails. We don't geo-fence by email domain (only by pin location), so they could use the app. One of them actually drove to Berkeley to pick up a mini fridge. First sign of cross-campus potential, even if it's silly.

## Next Steps

Based on pilot data, the priorities for v2 are:
1. **Real search** (Algolia integration  -  search was the #1 complaint)
2. **Relist flow** (one-tap relist for expired/failed-handoff items)
3. **Bundle listings** (list 5 items as a package deal)
4. **Content moderation** (Google Vision API for photo screening)
5. **Multi-campus** (UCLA and Stanford next, partner with student orgs there)

Target: ready for Fall 2024 move-in at Berkeley + 2 new campuses.
